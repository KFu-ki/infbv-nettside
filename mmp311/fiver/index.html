Process model · FIVER

From understanding to response

FIVER organises issue and risk work as a recurring process. New information can send the organisation back to any earlier stage.

Work through the five stagesCompare the models

The five stages

  1. F

    Frame and understand

    Establish what is happening, how the matter is defined, what remains uncertain and which assumptions structure the first interpretation.

  2. I

    Identify

    Identify stakeholders, expectations, weak signals, values, vulnerabilities and possible developments.

  3. V

    Evaluate

    Assess significance, urgency, uncertainty and organisational capacity. Use PRK and SOFT to challenge the initial picture.

  4. E

    Revise

    Change policy, priorities, routines, resources or coordination before treating communication as the solution.

  5. R

    Respond

    Act and communicate. Explain decisions and uncertainty, listen to feedback, evaluate the effects and begin the cycle again.

What FIVER is, and what it is not

A teaching and analytical framework

FIVER combines established issues-management work with risk and crisis communication. It helps students and organisations structure a case without reducing it to a checklist.

Not a validation claim

Pedagogical use shows that the framework has been tested and revised in teaching. It does not establish empirical validity or guarantee a successful response.

Full teaching text

Risk analysis and scenario development with FIVER

FIVER is a recurring process of framing and understanding, identifying, evaluating, revising and responding. Learning, trust-building and expectation management remain active throughout the cycle.

Frame and understand

Precaution begins with early situational awareness. Organisations should systematically gather information from professional environments, communities, media and relevant experts. Stakeholder mapping and attention to weak signals can make emerging problems visible before they escalate.

Identify

The organisation identifies concrete problems and contested issues that may develop into risks or crises. It must separate relevant signals from noise, prioritise matters requiring action and avoid dismissing unclear issues merely because their consequences remain uncertain.

Evaluate

Each identified matter is assessed in relation to possible consequences, uncertainty, vulnerability and organisational capacity. Probability and consequence may inform the assessment, but cannot replace judgement. Scenarios help decision-makers examine plausible developments, demanding conditions and necessary preparedness.

Revise

Analysis must lead to practical change. Organisations may need to revise preparedness plans, roles, resources, routines, coordination or policy. Communication cannot compensate for a material or organisational problem that remains unchanged.

Respond

Response requires defined roles, rapid notification, coordinated information and honest communication about decisions and uncertainty. Stakeholder involvement continues before, during and after the event. Evaluation turns experience into revised procedures and begins the cycle again.

FIVER was developed and revised through teaching at Volda University College, NTNU and HAW Kiel, work with crisis exercises, and research on municipal preparedness. Pedagogical use does not constitute a claim of empirical validation.
