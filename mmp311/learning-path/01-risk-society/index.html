Risk, uncertainty and the social production of relevance

Risk is not only a measurable property of a hazard. Risk also depends on what a society values, what experts can make visible, what institutions are prepared to recognise, and which consequences receive public attention.

Danger, risk and uncertainty

A danger may exist independently of whether an organisation has assessed it. Risk describes a possible development in relation to valued outcomes. Probability and consequence can support judgement, but they do not remove uncertainty. Ambiguity arises when actors agree on facts but disagree about values, responsibility or acceptable action.

Beck and the risk society

Ulrich Beck’s argument concerns modernity’s capacity to produce hazards that cross institutional and geographical boundaries. Scientific and technological development does not simply reduce danger. It also creates new dependencies, new uncertainty and disputes about who is authorised to define risk.

The analytical task is therefore not to repeat that contemporary society is risky. It is to examine how a risk becomes knowable, whose knowledge counts, who carries the consequences and how institutions respond when established categories no longer fit.

Student activity

Choose one current case. Separate the observable danger, the assessed risk, the remaining uncertainty and the competing value judgements. Identify what would change if another stakeholder defined the situation.
