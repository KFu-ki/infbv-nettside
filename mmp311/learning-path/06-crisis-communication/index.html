Crisis communication as part of crisis management

Author’s adapted translation. This resource reorganises and expands selected arguments from Brataas, Fuglseth, and Bakken (2026), Etterpåklok på forhånd, chapter 4. It is not a reproduction of the published chapter.

Crisis communication is part of crisis management. Information affects safety, coordination, legitimacy and the organisation’s capacity to act.

Before, during and after

Before a crisis, the organisation maps scenarios, stakeholders, roles, channels and fallback systems, and practises difficult decisions. During a crisis, it communicates what is known, what remains unknown, what people should do and when the next update will come. After the acute phase, communication supports explanation, accountability, learning and long-term relations with affected groups.

Principles under pressure

Speed, clarity, openness and responsibility are practical demands, not slogans. Speed without verification can cause harm. Openness has legal and ethical constraints. Clarity must not conceal uncertainty. Responsibility requires visible decisions and correction, not merely a visible spokesperson.

Media, news criteria and framing

Journalists select stories through significance, identification, sensation, timeliness and conflict, as well as elite actors, proximity and exclusivity. News values explain attention, not social importance. Media twist and framing help analyse how crisis coverage sharpens, simplifies, personalises and attributes responsibility. Organisations cannot control this process through message discipline alone.

Plans, stakeholders and alternatives

A crisis communication plan should connect strategic principles with roles, mobilisation, situational awareness, internal communication, external communication, monitoring, technology, templates and evaluation. It must include alternatives for loss of power, networks and internal systems, and consider language, disability and unequal access.

Practice and learning

Crisis communication is a practical discipline. Exercises should test incomplete information, media pressure, internal needs, affected groups, social media traffic and coordination with other responders. Evaluation should separate learning from blame and assign responsibility for actual change.

Student activity

Draft the first public update for a developing crisis. Include one verified fact, one explicit uncertainty, one protective action, one accountability statement and a time for the next update.
