Crisis communication as part of crisis management

MMP311 · Version 1.1 · Revised 19th of November, 2026 by Kristian Fuglseth (opens in a new tab)
Author’s adapted translation. This resource reorganises and expands selected arguments from the crisis communication chapter in Brataas, Bakken, and Fuglseth (2026), Etterpåklok på forhånd. It is not a reproduction of the published chapter.

Learning outcomes

  • Distinguish crisis information from crisis communication.
  • Plan internal and external communication as connected work.
  • Write an initial update under uncertainty.
  • Prioritise stakeholders, channels and fallback channels.
  • Evaluate communication as part of crisis management.

Crisis communication is part of crisis management. Information affects safety, coordination, legitimacy and the organisation’s capacity to act.

Crisis information and crisis communication

Crisis information distributes verified facts and instructions. Crisis communication is broader: it connects decision-making, listening, coordination, interpretation, uncertainty and relations with affected groups. Internal communication must begin before or alongside external communication because employees, leaders and operational teams need a shared basis for action.

Before, during and after

Before a crisis, the organisation maps scenarios, stakeholders, responsibilities, channels and fallback systems, and practises difficult decisions. During a crisis, it should communicate what is known, what is not known, what is being done, what people should do and when the next update will come. After the acute phase, communication supports explanation, accountability, learning and long-term relations.

Principles under pressure

Speed, clarity, openness and responsibility are practical demands, not slogans. Speed without verification can cause harm. Openness has legal and ethical limits. Clarity must not conceal uncertainty. Responsibility requires visible decisions, correction and follow-up, not merely a visible spokesperson.

Media, news criteria and framing

News values help explain attention, not social importance. Significance, identification, sensation, timeliness, conflict, elite actors, proximity and exclusivity shape selection. Hernes’s media distortion and framing theory help analyse how crisis coverage sharpens, simplifies, personalises and attributes responsibility. Organisations cannot control this process through message discipline alone.

Plans, stakeholders and alternative channels

A crisis communication plan should connect principles with roles, mobilisation, situational awareness, internal communication, external communication, monitoring, technology, templates and evaluation. Alternative channels are required for loss of power, networks and internal systems. Plans must account for language, disability, unequal access and the needs of directly affected groups.

Exercises and learning

Exercises should test incomplete information, media pressure, internal needs, affected stakeholders, social media traffic, inter-organisational coordination and technological failure. Evaluation should separate learning from blame and assign responsibility for actual change.

Minimum crisis communication plan

  1. Mandate and principles.
  2. Roles, authority and mobilisation thresholds.
  3. Situational awareness and verification.
  4. Internal communication.
  5. External stakeholders and accessibility.
  6. Channel priorities and fallback channels.
  7. Media relations and social media monitoring.
  8. Templates, logging and approval.
  9. Evaluation and assigned follow-up.

AI-supported work

AI may support transcription, translation, monitoring and drafting, but output requires human verification. Crisis use must protect personal data, sensitive operational information and information security. Synthetic images, automated spokespersons and unverified summaries can undermine both accuracy and accountability.

Worked example

A service interruption begins with incomplete information. The first update should identify the verified disruption, name what remains unknown, state protective action, assign responsibility and provide the time of the next update.

Two initial updates

Useful initial update

We are investigating a disruption affecting the municipal water supply in the eastern service area. The cause and duration are not yet confirmed. Residents in the affected streets should not use tap water for drinking or food preparation until further notice. Bottled water is available at the community centre from 14:00. The municipality will publish the next update by 15:30, even if the situation has not changed.

The update separates verified information from uncertainty, gives protective action, identifies support and commits to a next update.

Poor initial update

We are aware of a situation and have everything under control. More information will follow.

The message provides no scope, evidence, action, accountability or time for the next update. “Under control” is an unsupported reassurance.

Student activity

Draft the first public update for a developing crisis. Include one verified fact, one explicit uncertainty, one protective action, one accountability statement and the time of the next update.

Common misunderstanding

Speed does not justify unverified information. Openness does not require disclosure of protected personal data or sensitive operational information.

Expected output

An initial public update, an internal situation note, a stakeholder map, a fallback-channel plan and two evaluation criteria.
