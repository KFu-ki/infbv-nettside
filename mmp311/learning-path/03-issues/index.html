Issue detection and problem prevention

MMP311 · Version 1.1 · Last academically revised 19 November 2026 · Responsible academic: Kristian Fuglseth

Learning outcomes

  • Explain why an issue precedes a possible problem.
  • Identify who makes a matter relevant and on what grounds.
  • Separate investigation and adaptation from organisational defence.
  • Analyse how journalistic techniques affect visibility.

An issue begins when an actor makes a matter relevant by questioning conduct, consequences, values or responsibility. Early issues management examines the claim before the organisation defines the matter as a problem.

Issue before problem

An issue may be clarified, accommodated or resolved before it produces a problem. It becomes a problem when it creates an expectation gap, obstructs legitimate goals, harms stakeholders or places the organisation under sustained pressure to act. Early work therefore asks who points to the issue, which experience or evidence supports the claim, and what the organisation can clarify or change.

Possible responses include clarification, participation, adaptation, changes in policy or practice, and a reasoned rejection of a claim. Issues management is not organisational self-defence. It is a systematic way to test whether conduct, consequences, values or responsibility require action.

How an issue becomes visible

Hernes’s concept of media distortion (medievridning) identifies accentuation, simplification, polarisation, intensification, concretisation and personification as techniques that shape attention. These techniques do not automatically create the underlying problem. They affect what becomes visible, how responsibility is attributed and how quickly an organisation must respond.

Worked example

A local group questions a proposed service change. The issue exists when the claim is made relevant. It becomes an organisational problem only if the proposal creates an unresolved expectation gap, material harm or sustained conflict.

Student activity

Select an emerging issue. State what can still be clarified before it becomes a problem. Identify the conduct or non-response that could move the issue towards risk or crisis, and propose one preventive organisational change.

Common misunderstanding

Media attention documents visibility, not proof that the organisation caused the issue. The underlying claim, conduct and consequences require separate evidence.

Expected output

A one-page issue brief identifying the claim, evidence, stakeholders, expectation gap, clarification needs and possible organisational change.

Using generative AI in this learning resource

Generative AI can support brainstorming, search planning, language revision, comparison and self-testing. It does not replace reading, source retrieval, disciplinary judgement or writing. Language models generate probable text and may present incorrect facts, references and interpretations. Verify every factual claim and reference in the original source.

  • Do not upload sensitive, confidential or copyrighted material to open AI services.
  • Check who provides the tool and what happens to submitted data.
  • Use Oria and academic databases for quality-assured literature searches; AI tools may supplement, but do not replace, a reproducible search.
  • Describe which AI tool you used, for what purpose, and what remained your own work.
  • Check the current rules for the course and institution before submitting assessed work.

Read the full Søk & Skriv guidance on artificial intelligence.

Useful AI exercise: Use AI as a critical counterparty to propose alternative stakeholder perspectives. Verify the perspectives against actual documents or stakeholder material before using them in an issue brief.
