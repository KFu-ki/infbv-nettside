Issue detection and problem prevention

An issue can be pointed out before it becomes a problem. Early issue work asks whether a development, expectation or disagreement requires clarification, change or dialogue.

Issue before problem

An issue is a matter that an actor makes relevant. The issue may be resolved, clarified or accommodated before it produces material consequences or sustained conflict. It becomes a problem when it creates an expectation gap, obstructs legitimate goals, harms stakeholders or places the organisation under pressure to act.

Issues management should therefore not be reduced to defending the organisation. Its first task is to listen: who points to the issue, what experience or evidence supports the claim, and what could the organisation change before the matter develops further?

How issues become visible

Journalistic selection can sharpen an issue through accentuation, simplification, polarisation, intensification, concretisation and personification. These techniques do not automatically create the underlying problem. They influence which aspects become visible and how quickly the organisation must respond.

Student activity

Analyse an emerging issue. State what could still be clarified before it becomes a problem. Then identify the conduct or non-response that could move the issue towards risk or crisis.
