FIVER: from interpretation to organisational change

FIVER organises issue and risk work as a recurring process. It is an author-developed teaching and analytical framework, not a claim of predictive or empirical validation.

Five stages

  1. Frame and understand. Establish the situation, actors, knowledge, assumptions and uncertainty.
  2. Identify. Locate issues, expectation gaps, weak signals, vulnerabilities and relevant stakeholders.
  3. Evaluate. Assess possible consequences, uncertainty, capacity and alternative interpretations. Use PRK and SOFT.
  4. Revise. Change practice, policy, priorities, resources, routines or coordination. Communication cannot compensate for an unchanged material problem.
  5. Respond. Act, communicate, listen, coordinate and evaluate. New information may return the process to any earlier stage.

Student activity

Use FIVER on a municipal preparedness case. The response must identify at least one organisational change that should occur before communication is treated as the solution.
