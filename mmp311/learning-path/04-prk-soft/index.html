PRK and SOFT: movement, attention and interpretation

MMP311 · Version 1.1 · Last academically revised 19 November 2026 · Responsible academic: Kristian Fuglseth

Learning outcomes

  • Use PRK without assuming automatic escalation.
  • Recognise new issues, problems and risks created by crisis response.
  • Use SOFT to identify blind spots in a situation analysis.
  • Distinguish media distortion, framing, media logic and mediatization.

PRK locates conditional movement between problem, risk and crisis. SOFT challenges what the first situation analysis has made visible.

Issue detection before PRK

An issue stands before the PRK triad. It may be clarified without becoming a problem. If the matter exposes an expectation gap, material harm or sustained conflict, it may reveal or produce a problem. A problem may create risk without becoming a crisis, while crisis response may create new problems and risks.

SOFT as a critical lens

The resource uses SOFT as the name of a teaching lens rather than as a strict letter-by-letter initialism. It tests social conditions, opinions and expectations, framing, transparency and organisational conditions. The lens asks whose exposure and knowledge are visible, which interpretation dominates, what the organisation can explain, and whether it has authority and capacity to change.

Media distortion, framing, media logic and mediatization

These concepts operate at different analytical levels. Media distortion concerns concrete presentation techniques. Framing concerns selection, problem definition, causal interpretation, responsibility and remedies. Media logic concerns technological, aesthetic and institutional working conditions. Mediatization concerns longer-term changes in the relationship between media and other institutions. The levels can be connected analytically, but they do not constitute a single documented causal chain.

Worked example

A technical disruption may be handled without public controversy. If service users experience unequal consequences, the disruption becomes a problem. Poor response may create legitimacy risk, while an acute operational breakdown may become a crisis and generate new issues.

Student activity

Apply PRK and SOFT to the same case. First locate the movement. Then identify what the first account misses because of social position, framing, transparency or organisational limits.

Common misunderstanding

PRK is not a timeline and SOFT is not a substitute for technical risk assessment. PRK locates relations; SOFT tests what the first analysis excludes.

Expected output

A two-part analysis: one PRK map and one SOFT table showing what the first map overlooked.

Using generative AI in this learning resource

Generative AI can support brainstorming, search planning, language revision, comparison and self-testing. It does not replace reading, source retrieval, disciplinary judgement or writing. Language models generate probable text and may present incorrect facts, references and interpretations. Verify every factual claim and reference in the original source.

  • Do not upload sensitive, confidential or copyrighted material to open AI services.
  • Check who provides the tool and what happens to submitted data.
  • Use Oria and academic databases for quality-assured literature searches; AI tools may supplement, but do not replace, a reproducible search.
  • Describe which AI tool you used, for what purpose, and what remained your own work.
  • Check the current rules for the course and institution before submitting assessed work.

Read the full Søk & Skriv guidance on artificial intelligence.

Useful AI exercise: Let AI challenge an initial PRK placement or SOFT analysis. Treat the output as possible questions, not evidence. Decisions about framing, responsibility and organisational capacity require checked material.
