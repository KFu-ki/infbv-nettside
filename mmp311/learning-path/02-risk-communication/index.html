Risk communication as knowledge and trust work

Risk communication is knowledge work under conditions where facts, uncertainty, values and practical action must be brought into relation.

Four purposes that may conflict

Risk communication can inform, support protective action, build warranted trust and create a forum for disagreement. These purposes do not always align. A simplified message may make action easier while hiding uncertainty. Open deliberation may reveal value conflict that cannot be solved by supplying more information.

Expertise, publics and trust

Expertise does not speak outside a rhetorical situation. Audiences assess competence, integrity and goodwill through language, institutional affiliation, previous conduct and the networks supporting an expert claim. Trust should not be treated as a communication target detached from conduct. Communication can support warranted trust only when decisions, limitations and uncertainty are explained.

Student activity

Compare an expert message with a public response to the same risk. Identify what each side treats as evidence, what remains uncertain, and which expectations are not answered.
