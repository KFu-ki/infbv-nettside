Risk communication as knowledge and trust work

MMP311 · Course year 2027 · Version 1.2 · First published 2026 · Last academically revised 19 November 2026 · Responsible academic: Kristian Fuglseth

Learning outcomes

  • Distinguish risk assessment, risk perception and risk communication.
  • Identify care, consensus and crisis communication.
  • Assess whether trust is warranted by competence, integrity and conduct.
  • Use SOFT to identify social and organisational blind spots.

Risk communication connects assessment, public interpretation, values and practical action. It cannot be reduced to transferring expert information to a passive audience.

Assessment, perception and communication

Risk assessment evaluates possible harm, uncertainty and vulnerability. Risk perception concerns how actors interpret relevance, credibility, fairness and control. Risk communication is the exchange through which knowledge, uncertainty, values and possible action are made available for scrutiny and decision.

Three communication situations

Care communication
Supports informed choices and protective action where the hazard is reasonably established.
Consensus communication
Addresses disagreement about decisions, values, distribution and acceptable risk.
Crisis communication
Supports urgent action and coordination under time pressure, uncertainty and possible harm.

The situations may overlap. A crisis can contain long-standing value conflict, and care communication may fail when unequal resources make recommended action unrealistic.

Expertise, ethos and warranted trust

Expert knowledge enters a particular rhetorical situation. Audiences assess not only the information, but also the expert, the institution, previous conduct and the interests surrounding the claim. Trust should not be treated as an attitude that communication can manufacture. Communication supports warranted trust when competence, integrity, goodwill, uncertainty and institutional limitations are visible.

Use SOFT to test the communication

SOFT asks whether the communication accounts for social conditions, opinions and expectations, framing, transparency and organisational conditions. The lens helps distinguish an information deficit from a conflict about values, unequal consequences or organisational conduct.

Worked example

A drinking-water warning combines crisis communication with care communication. Residents need immediate protective advice, but the authority must also explain uncertainty, unequal access to alternatives and when new information will be available.

Student activity

Compare an expert message and a public response concerning the same risk. Identify what each treats as evidence, which uncertainty is acknowledged, which values are contested, and which recommendations assume resources that some audiences may not have.

Common misunderstanding

Being open about uncertainty is not the same as being vague. Useful uncertainty communication states what is known, what is not known, why the limitation matters and what will happen next.

Expected output

A short risk message containing known evidence, explicit uncertainty, recommended action, justification and an accessibility check.

Using generative AI in this learning resource

Generative AI can support brainstorming, search planning, language revision, comparison and self-testing. It does not replace reading, source retrieval, disciplinary judgement or writing. Language models generate probable text and may present incorrect facts, references and interpretations. Verify every factual claim and reference in the original source.

  • Do not upload sensitive, confidential or copyrighted material to open AI services.
  • Check who provides the tool and what happens to submitted data.
  • Use Oria and academic databases for quality-assured literature searches; AI tools may supplement, but do not replace, a reproducible search.
  • Describe which AI tool you used, for what purpose, and what remained your own work.
  • Check the current rules for the course and institution before submitting assessed work.

Read the full Search & Write guidance on artificial intelligence.

Useful AI exercise: Compare alternative formulations of a risk message, then assess whether each version makes evidence, uncertainty, recommended action and unequal capacity visible. The final assessment must be your own.
