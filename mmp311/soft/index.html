Four connected fields of attention

Social conditions

Who is exposed, excluded or dependent on the organisation? Consider unequal resources, language, access, vulnerability and local relationships.

Opinions and expectations

What do stakeholders expect, and where do expectations conflict? Public reaction is not a substitute for evidence, but it can reveal contested values.

Framing

Which interpretation dominates? Frames select causes, consequences, responsibility and possible solutions while other perspectives remain less visible.

Transparency and organisational conditions

What can the organisation know, share and change? Examine information flow, authority, culture, resources and willingness to correct the initial understanding.

Questions for analysis

Whose situation?

Which groups carry the consequences, and which voices are missing from the organisation's picture?

Whose expectations?

What do different stakeholders consider reasonable, legitimate or safe?

Whose frame?

How do language, images, sources and categories define the matter?

Which capacity?

Can the organisation listen, coordinate, explain uncertainty and change established practice?

SOFT as a critical analytical lens

Technical risk assessment cannot by itself explain how an issue becomes socially important. SOFT supplements probability and consequence analysis by examining social conditions, opinions, framing, transparency and organisational conditions.

Social conditions

Analysis starts with the conditions under which people encounter the issue. Exposure, resources, language, access, dependency and vulnerability are unevenly distributed. The same event may therefore have very different consequences for different groups.

Opinions and expectations

Stakeholder opinions are not substitutes for evidence. They can, however, reveal contested values, unmet expectations and emerging conflicts. Organisations must distinguish between disagreement about facts, disagreement about values and disagreement about what constitutes a legitimate response.

Framing

Frames select aspects of a matter and connect them to causes, consequences, responsibility and possible solutions. Analysis must ask which frame dominates, whose knowledge supports it and which alternatives remain less visible.

Transparency and organisational conditions

An organisation's ability to act and communicate depends on authority, information flows, professional culture, resources and coordination. Transparency requires more than publishing information. The organisation must explain uncertainty, identify limitations and show what it is prepared to change.

SOFT is used as an author-developed teaching approach in risk, crisis and issue communication. The resource draws on teaching at Volda University College, NTNU and HAW Kiel, crisis exercises and municipal research.

Responsible use of AI

AI can support questions, comparison and language revision, but it does not replace reading, evidence or professional judgement. Verify claims, protect sensitive material and disclose relevant use. Read the Søk & Skriv guidance.
